Product Design

35% faster sales in POS

Complete redesign of a Point of Sale for day to day activities in cultural institutions and non-profits.

Role
Product Designer
Team
Product, Customer Success & Engineering
Timeline
6 months
My focus
Modernizing the interface, systems and accessibility behind a faster, multi-platform POS
01

At a glance

Problem
The existing POS was slow, hard to scale, and difficult for the many older volunteer staff to use — small text and touch targets that were easy to miss made every sale take longer.
Insight
Staff couldn't find features that already existed, task-time data showed real friction, and engineering had to rebuild the same feature twice — once for desktop, once for iPad.
Impact
Staff testing showed noticeably faster ticket and membership sales.
↗ +35% faster sales
02

Context — why it matters

The POS is where a museum's day-to-day actually happens: ticket sales, memberships, donations, souvenirs, check-ins — usually run by a small team of volunteers, many of them older adults, doing their best to keep the line moving. When it works well, it's not just staff who benefit — supporters get their tickets and memberships faster, with clearer information, right at the front desk.

03

The problem, with evidence

Recorded task times showed staff were about 30% slower than expected when selling items. Over 45 support cases came in from more than 35 museums describing the same friction. Beyond speed, the interface itself worked against the people using it day to day: dense product grids with little breathing room, low-contrast secondary text, and icon-only controls with no visible label — real obstacles for a staff group that skews older and less tech-fluent.

50% less churn in CRM

Merging 5 legacy platforms into one product ecosystem, powered by a shared design system.

Role
Lead UX Designer
Team

Timeline
4 months
My focus
Unifying 5 legacy Veevart platforms into one ecosystem through a shared design system, bringing runaway churn back under control
01

At a glance

Problem
Veevart's legacy platforms were outdated and inconsistent in their components and flows across all products — and it was costing them clients, with churn running at double the expected rate.
Insight
Veevart lacked a design system that simplified and gave direction to its products.
Impact
One ecosystem instead of five platforms — churn cut in half, back to the expected rate — plus 40% higher adoption and 15% less onboarding time, all delivered in 4 months.
02

Context — why it matters

Led the end-to-end UX/UI design of the design system, focused on making components easy to use across multiple platforms. Worked closely with developers and PMs to understand each product's needs. The company was carrying technical debt, facing pressure to match competitors' quality, and needed to reinvent itself to satisfy investors.

03

The problem, with evidence

Veevart was seeing churn at double the expected rate — more than half of those cases tied to confusing flows and the fact that the same task worked differently depending on which part of the platform you were in. Over 20 clients raised leaving for simpler competitor platforms, citing both the confusing flows and how visually dated Veevart felt in comparison.

30% less drop-off in online sales

Redesigning Veevart's customer-facing pages — ticketing, donations, and membership signup — for the people using them, not just the staff behind them.

Role
Product Designer
Team
Product, PMs & Engineering
Timeline
5 months
My focus
Redesigning Veevart's end-user pages for accessibility and ease of use across a wide range of visitors
01

At a glance

Problem
Veevart's customer-facing pages were outdated, unattractive, and had real usability problems — and their aging codebase made it hard to add the functionality customers were asking for.
Insight
The average museum visitor was a student or a parent — very different kinds of users — so the platform needed to be accessible, visually appealing, and easy to use for the widest possible range of people.
Impact
Positive reception from museums so far; over 160 clients will begin rolling these pages out to their thousands of customers over the coming year.
02

Context — why it matters

Led the end-to-end UX/UI design of Veevart's customer-facing pages, working with the same product, PM, and engineering teams as the design system project. The focus shifted from internal staff tools to the pages thousands of museum visitors interact with directly.

03

The problem, with evidence

The old checkout required filling in every member of a family membership upfront, and didn't clearly show what each membership included before asking visitors to commit. On the ticketing side, buyers had no time-slot selector inside each event, and had to go through a separate cart-review step before reaching checkout.

There was a consistent drop-off point in family memberships when visitors were asked to add each member upfront, and a similar drop-off in ticketing right at the old "Review cart" step before checkout. Reducing that drop-off was one of the central goals of this redesign.

05

Design principles

— Conversion

"Every field costs a visitor"

Requested information had to earn its place at checkout, or it got moved out of the way.

Veevart Apps · Design principle

"Remove the step, don't just redesign it"

The old cart-review step didn't get a facelift — it got eliminated once time slots lived inside the event itself.

Veevart Apps · Design principle
— Efficiency

"Reuse what already works"

Letting members manage their own family list through the existing portal, instead of building something new from scratch.

Veevart Apps · Design principle
— User focus

"Design for whoever's paying, not just whoever's staff"

Built around students and parents — very different visitors — not around internal power users.

Veevart Apps · Design principle

Key decisions

Adopt, don't reinvent

Some teams had to redesign their customer-facing flows around a pattern that had already proven itself elsewhere in the system, rather than building something new from scratch.
